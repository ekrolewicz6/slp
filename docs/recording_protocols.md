# Recording Protocols

**Date:** 2026-04-30
**Status:** draft scripts for MVP recorder and prospective pilots

These protocols are designed for consistent language-state measurement, not
diagnosis. They avoid names, dates of birth, addresses, school names, medical
record numbers, and other identifying details.

## Universal Setup

Before recording:

1. Use a participant pseudonym.
2. Enter age, not date of birth.
3. Confirm the participant should not say their name.
4. Choose the task.
5. Place microphone 1-2 feet from the speaker when possible.
6. Reduce background noise.
7. Record one short test clip and check that speech is audible.

If a task requires playback, use headphones or a second device layout that does
not cause the prompt audio to dominate the participant's microphone channel.

## Adult Aphasia Protocol

### A1. Conversation / Interview

**Goal:** natural functional language, repair behavior, fluency, and content.

**Prompt options:**

- "Tell me about something you did recently."
- "Tell me about a place you know well."
- "Tell me about a meal, hobby, or routine you enjoy."

**Instructions to recorder:**

- Ask one open-ended prompt.
- Use neutral backchannels.
- Avoid correcting the participant during the sample.
- If meaning is unclear, ask one explicit clarification question and mark that
  the sample included support.

**Target length:** 3-5 minutes.

### A2. Picture Description

**Goal:** task-conditioned expected concepts and informativeness.

**Prompt:**

"Look at this picture. Tell me everything you see happening."

**Instructions:**

- Do not name the expected concepts.
- Allow silence.
- If the participant stops quickly, say: "Anything else?"

**Target length:** 1-3 minutes.

### A3. Narrative / Story Retell

**Goal:** discourse organization, event sequencing, content, and repairability.

**Prompt:**

"Tell me the story of Cinderella as best you can."

Alternative if culturally unfamiliar:

"Tell me a familiar story or movie from beginning to end."

**Target length:** 3-7 minutes.

### A4. Repetition Probe

**Goal:** phonological encoding and structured production.

**Prompt:**

"I will say a sentence. Please repeat it exactly."

**Example sentence levels:**

- short: "The boy is running."
- medium: "The woman put the keys on the table."
- complex: "Before the man opened the window, the dog barked loudly."

**Note:** use only if the protocol allows repetition and the prompt audio can be
separated enough for scoring.

## Child Language / DLD Protocol

### C1. Natural Conversation / Play

**Goal:** ecologically valid language sample.

**Prompt options:**

- play with a small set of toys;
- talk about a favorite game, show, or activity;
- describe what happened today.

**Recorder behavior:**

- Follow the child's lead.
- Use open-ended prompts.
- Avoid testing-style corrections.
- Do not provide target words unless needed to keep the interaction going.

**Target length:** 5-10 minutes.

### C2. Picture Description

**Prompt:**

"Tell me what is happening in this picture."

**Follow-up:**

"Anything else?"

**Target length:** 1-3 minutes.

### C3. Story Retell

**Prompt:**

"I am going to tell you a short story. Then you tell it back to me."

**Requirements:**

- Use a fixed script.
- Keep prompt audio separate from child audio if possible.
- Record whether the story was heard live or from playback.

**Target length:** 2-5 minutes.

### C4. Sentence Repetition

**Prompt:**

"Say exactly what I say."

**Administration:**

- Start with short sentences.
- Stop if the child is distressed or refuses.
- Mark whether repetitions were live or played from device.

**Why it matters:** Brian emphasized sentence repetition as a tighter task that
can reveal language difficulty when conversation is too unconstrained.

### C5. Nonword Repetition

**Prompt:**

"These are silly made-up words. Say them just like I say them."

**Administration:**

- Use a standardized nonword list from an approved protocol.
- Do not invent ad hoc items for research scoring.
- Mark audio quality carefully.

**Why it matters:** nonword repetition can probe phonological memory and has
been linked to language disorder and stuttering persistence/recovery questions.

### C6. Parent / Teacher Functional Context

**Questions:**

- "What communication situation is hardest right now?"
- "What has improved recently?"
- "What is the main goal for the next month?"

**Note:** store as optional structured context, not as a diagnostic label.

## Stuttering Protocol

### S1. Natural Conversation

**Goal:** spontaneous fluency profile and language-fluency interaction.

**Prompt options:**

- "Tell me about something you like to do."
- "Tell me about school or work."
- "Tell me about something exciting or frustrating that happened recently."

**Target length:** 5 minutes.

### S2. Reading

**Goal:** compare spontaneous speech with a controlled text condition.

**Instructions:**

- Use age-appropriate passage.
- Mark passage ID.
- Do not interrupt.

### S3. Sentence Production / Repetition

**Goal:** test whether syntactic length/complexity changes disfluency rate.

**Prompt:**

"Repeat this sentence exactly."

or

"Make a sentence using these words."

**Note:** separate prompt audio from participant audio when possible.

### S4. Impact / Context Rating

**Questions:**

- "How hard was this speaking task?"
- "Do some situations make speaking easier or harder?"
- "Is there anything you avoided saying?"

For children, collect parent/caregiver context where appropriate.

## Minimal Metadata Per Task

Every task recording should include:

- participant pseudonym;
- age in months or years;
- language(s);
- population/domain;
- task ID;
- task type;
- prompt/script version;
- recording device;
- sample rate/channels;
- recording duration;
- whether prompt audio is present in the recording;
- whether support/clarification was given;
- whether spoken PHI may be present.

## Stop Rules

Stop or skip a task if:

- participant is distressed;
- participant asks to stop;
- privacy risk becomes obvious;
- environment is too noisy for interpretable recording;
- recording device fails.

## Scoring Boundary

Raw transcript/audio can support measurement after appropriate validation.

ASR, LLM cleanup, and reconstructed outputs can support communication and review,
but they must not be scored as independent patient-produced language.
