# Clinical Literature Matrix

This matrix is a decision aid, not a full systematic review. It records the papers and public sources that most directly change the experiment plan.

| domain | priority | paper/source | clinical finding | implication for this project | source |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DLD diagnosis | 1 | Bishop, Snowling, Thompson, Greenhalgh, and CATALISE Consortium (2016), CATALISE phase 1 | The field lacked agreement on how to identify child language impairment; consensus emphasized functional impact and need for specialist help rather than one cutoff. | Treat DLD/TD labels as noisy supervision; build state variables with uncertainty. | https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0158753 |
| DLD terminology | 1 | Bishop, Snowling, Thompson, Greenhalgh, and CATALISE-2 Consortium (2017), CATALISE phase 2 | DLD terminology is broad and heterogeneous by design, with known biomedical conditions separated from DLD. | Do not make a direct DLD classifier the central claim; model grammar, lexicon, narrative/content, fluency, and learning response. | https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12721 |
| DLD prevalence/fairness | 1 | Norbury et al. (2016), SCALES prevalence/nonverbal ability | Nonverbal IQ exclusions change who is counted and served. | Audit models for cognitive, demographic, bilingual, and corpus confounds; SCALES is a top access target. | https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12573 |
| DLD structured tasks | 1 | SCALES sentence repetition marker paper | Sentence repetition remains a robust DLD marker in a population-derived cohort. | Add a structured-task axis; test what sentence repetition adds beyond natural speech. | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13086201/ |
| DLD structured tasks | 1 | Fiveash et al. (2023), rhythmic primes and sentence repetition in DLD | Regular rhythmic primes improved sentence repetition in children with DLD. | Run the Fiveash OSF pilot; ask whether rhythm exposes a modifiable state, not only a severity score. | https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00170-1 |
| DLD structured tasks | 2 | Kueser and Leonard (2020), frequency/predictability in repetition | Sentence repetition depends on frequency and predictability, not just global language ability. | Model item-level psycholinguistic features; avoid treating repetition as a single black-box number. | https://doi.org/10.1044/2019_JSLHR-19-00155 |
| DLD markers | 2 | Paradis (2013), nonword repetition and tense | Nonword repetition and tense markers capture partly separable weaknesses. | The minimum battery should include natural speech, sentence repetition, and nonword repetition when possible. | https://talkbank.org/childes/access/Clinical-Eng/0docs/Paradis2013.pdf |
| DLD intervention | 1 | Calder, Claessen, Ebbels, and Leitao (2020), explicit grammar intervention SCED | Repeated probes can show target-specific gains across trained, untrained, extension, and control items. | Use the ASHA Figshare supplements for single-case response curves and target-generalization analysis. | https://doi.org/10.1044/2019_LSHSS-19-00060 |
| DLD intervention | 1 | Balthazar, Ebbels, Zwitserlood et al. (2020), explicit grammar intervention approaches | Grammar treatment often combines explicit metalinguistic instruction, stimulus organization, multimodal supports, and individualized targets. | Build target-selection models around probe difficulty, generalization, and response curves. | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7225018/ |
| DLD intervention | 2 | Efficacy of DLD treatment systematic review (2021) | Evidence is domain-specific; broad language scores can hide what changed. | Report construct-level outcomes rather than one global treatment-response score. | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8005159/ |
| DLD intervention | 1 | Roberts and Kaiser (2015), early intervention for toddlers with language delays RCT | Caregiver instruction changed caregiver language-facilitation strategies and produced short-term language effects. | Treat dose, fidelity, input, and target exposure as core variables, not metadata. | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4379460/ |
| DLD treatment data | 1 | Grauzer, Roberts, and Jones (2026), Dryad EMT-SF dataset | Public data include randomized assignment and repeated aggregate outcomes, but not raw transcript/audio or session-level targets/dose. | Use Dryad as a measurement-response bridge; request raw samples and target/dose metadata after summarizing aggregate results. | https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.sj3tx96g9 |
| DLD longitudinal | 1 | Manchester Language Study / Conti-Ramsden longitudinal corpus | DLD consequences extend into young adulthood across language, literacy, cognition, and functional outcomes. | Use Manchester after UKDS access to test whether early state predicts adult outcomes. | https://reshare.ukdataservice.ac.uk/852066/ |
| DLD longitudinal | 1 | SCALES intensive T2-T5 documentation | SCALES links repeated language, literacy, cognition, speech/hearing, mental-health, parent/teacher, and educational variables. | Prepare UKDS access; this is the strongest non-TalkBank DLD target. | https://doi.org/10.5255/UKDA-SN-8968-1 |
| Child language computation | 1 | Sagae, Lavie, and MacWhinney (2005), automatic syntactic development measurement | Parsed/learned child-language measures can outperform or complement hand-scored IPSyn-style methods. | Use classic measures as anchors, then learn reliable state dimensions from outcomes and robustness tests. | https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~alavie/papers/sagae-acl05-final.pdf |
| Child language computation | 1 | Sagae (2021), neural language models for child language development | Neural language models trained on CHILDES can track developmental state better than MLU and automated IPSyn in some settings. | The state model should compete against MLU, IPSyn, and KIDEVAL, not merely reproduce them. | https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.674402 |
| DLD screening | 2 | Automated DLD screening review (2025) | Automated DLD screening uses acoustic, text, and combined features, but validation remains heterogeneous. | Require corpus-held-out validation, balanced participant sampling, and construct-specific outputs. | https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/automated-approaches-to-screening-developmental-language-disorder/ |
| Stuttering natural history | 1 | Yairi and Ambrose early childhood stuttering recovery work | Many preschool children recover, but risk stratification matters because persistence risk varies. | Use FluencyBank for recovery-risk and timing models, not just CWS-vs-control classification. | https://doi.org/10.1044/jslhr.4205.1097 |
| Stuttering theory | 1 | Smith and Weber (2017), multifactorial dynamic pathways theory | Stuttering develops through interacting speech-motor, language, emotional, and environmental pathways. | Build longitudinal state models with disfluency, language growth, speech motor/acoustic timing, reactions, and family risk where available. | https://doi.org/10.1044/2017_JSLHR-S-16-0343 |
| Stuttering predictors | 1 | Spencer and Weber-Fox (2014), articulation and nonword repetition | Preschool articulation and nonword repetition may help distinguish recovery from persistence. | Add task indicators and structured probes where FluencyBank transcripts permit. | https://talkbank.org/fluency/access/0docs/spencer2014.pdf |
| Stuttering predictors | 1 | Walsh et al. (2020), disfluency characteristics at ages 4-5 | Weighted stuttering-like disfluency helps identify children at greater persistence risk. | Implement weighted SLD and compare to simple counts in IISRP, Purdue, Wagovich, and Ratner. | https://talkbank.org/fluency/access/0docs/walsh2020.pdf |
| Stuttering predictors | 1 | Predictive factors systematic review (2020) | Replicated predictors include phonological ability, articulatory rate, disfluency-pattern change, and one-year severity trend. | Make the next FluencyBank experiment a longitudinal-change model with corpus-held-out replication. | https://doi.org/10.1080/17549507.2020.1812718 |
| Stuttering treatment | 2 | Cochrane review of non-pharmacological treatments for stuttering in children <=6 years | The Lidcombe Program may reduce stuttering frequency versus wait-list, but certainty is limited. | Separate natural recovery prediction from treatment-response prediction. | https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD013489_medicine-free-treatment-stuttering-children-aged-six-years-and-younger |
| Stuttering corpus | 1 | Ratner and MacWhinney (2018), FluencyBank | FluencyBank enables shared, reproducible fluency research. | Now that access works, move from Purdue-only feasibility to all-local FluencyBank recovery modeling. | https://talkbank.org/fluency/access/0docs/ratner2018.pdf |
| Stuttering ASR | 2 | FluencyBank Timestamped / intended speech recognition work | Timestamped and enriched derivatives are being built for disfluency detection and intended-speech reconstruction. | Keep ASR assistive unless benchmarked against clinical disfluency labels and uncertainty. | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12379651/ |
| Aphasia dosage | 1 | RELEASE Collaborators and Brady et al. (2022), aphasia dosage/intensity IPD network meta-analysis | Dose, frequency, intensity, and duration relate differently to language, comprehension, and functional communication. | Model production, comprehension, functional communication, and discourse dimensions separately. | https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.121.035216 |
| Aphasia digital twins | 1 | Kiran et al. (2026), bilingual aphasia digital twin RCT | A computational model was prospectively tested in a double-blind RCT to choose therapy language. | This is the bar for a Nature-level clinical AI claim: prospective treatment assignment, not retrospective correlation. | https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-02583-9 |
| Aphasia discourse | 1 | Forbes, Fromm, and MacWhinney et al. (2022), Using AphasiaBank for discourse assessment | AphasiaBank supports main concept, core lexicon, CIU, and automated CLAN discourse analyses. | State reports should include content, core lexicon/main concepts, fluency, and acoustic/prosodic dimensions. | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8915263/ |
| Aphasia discourse | 1 | Fromm et al. (2024), FLUCALC | Automated fluency measurement quantifies clinically relevant discourse fluency features. | Integrate FLUCALC-like pausing, repetition, retracing, and filled-pause features. | https://talkbank.org/aphasia/publications/2024/Fromm24-fluency.pdf |
| Aphasia discourse | 1 | Pittman et al. (2025), CIU/discourse ML | The clinically useful unit is correct, relevant information, not just grammatical richness. | Keep content-state and known/unknown-intent risk as separate axes. | https://talkbank.org/aphasia/publications/2025/Pittman25.pdf |
| Aphasia multimodal | 2 | Frontiers 2024 multimodal aphasia discourse paper | Gesture, writing, drawing, AAC, and other modalities contribute to discourse success. | Future recorder/report designs should allow multimodal notes or video coding. | https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2024.1419311 |
| Aphasia GenAI | 1 | Adikari et al. (2025), reconstructing impaired language with generative AI | GPT-style models can reconstruct some aphasic utterances in controlled settings. | Use GenAI for assistive rewrite/clarify/abstain policies, not source-of-truth assessment. | https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-24725-x |
| Aphasia ASR | 2 | Automatic recognition and detection of aphasic natural speech (2024 preprint) | Natural aphasic speech can be classified from semi-automatic features, but ASR remains hard. | Continue ASR safety work; report conservative, unsafe, and human-confirmation cases separately. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14082 |
| Acoustic standards | 1 | Eyben et al. (2016), GeMAPS/eGeMAPS | Compact standardized acoustic features support reproducible speech research. | Keep openSMILE/eGeMAPS as the default acoustic replication baseline. | https://doi.org/10.1109/TAFFC.2015.2457417 |
